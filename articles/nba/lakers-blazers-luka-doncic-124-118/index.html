Luka Doncic Is Playing a Different Game Right Now: Lakers Survive Portland's Late Comeback

Matthew Robi November 7, 2026 14 min read

Portland had the better shooting night, Deni Avdija nearly recorded a triple-double, and the Blazers put up 43 points in the fourth quarter. None of it was enough to overcome a historic Luka Doncic performance.

Sometimes you watch a basketball game and come away thinking one team simply wasn't good enough.

Other times, you watch a basketball game and realize that one player was so ridiculously good that it almost doesn't matter what the other team did.

Friday night in Los Angeles was a little bit of the latter.

The Lakers defeated the Portland Trail Blazers 124-118, overcoming an early deficit and surviving a furious fourth-quarter comeback. Portland actually shot the ball better, made more threes, and assisted on considerably more baskets. But none of that could overcome the combination of 24 turnovers, defensive breakdowns, and, of course, Luka Doncic.

Luka finished the night with 53 points, five rebounds, and five assists.

And honestly, I'm not entirely sure what we're supposed to make of what he's doing right now.

Luka Doncic Is Entering Some Pretty Ridiculous Territory

Let's start with the obvious.

Luka Doncic was unbelievable.

He shot 17-of-33 from the field, knocked down five three-pointers, and got to the free-throw line 18 times. He scored from virtually everywhere on the floor and seemed completely comfortable regardless of what Portland threw at him.

There was even a moment at the end of the first half that perfectly captured the kind of player we're watching right now.

Luka launched a shot from 61 feet, well beyond the half-court line, and watched it drop through the net as the buzzer sounded.

And the funniest part? He barely reacted.

It was almost as if he knew it was going in before the ball even left his hands.

But here's where things get genuinely absurd.

Through his first nine appearances this season, Doncic has already scored 361 points. According to ESPN Research, the only player in NBA history to score more through his first nine games of a season is Wilt Chamberlain.

Read that again.

Wilt Chamberlain.

After Friday night's performance, Luka is averaging 40.1 points per game. He's also averaging 7.8 rebounds and 7.7 assists while shooting just under 50% from the field.

He's not simply leading the league in scoring. He's creating a gap between himself and everyone else that is difficult to comprehend.

The comparison that immediately comes to mind is prime James Harden in Houston. That version of Harden was capable of putting together scoring seasons that almost seemed statistically impossible. He was an entire offensive system by himself, and defenses knew exactly what was coming without being able to do much about it.

That's the territory Luka is approaching.

The difference is that Doncic can hurt you in so many different ways. He can score at all three levels, manipulate defensive coverages, create for teammates, and control the tempo of a game.

And while 40 points per game is obviously an unsustainable expectation over an entire season, what we're witnessing right now is historically special.

At some point, we have to stop treating these performances as just another great Luka game.

We're watching something extraordinary.

Scoot Henderson Is Still Searching for His Place in Portland's Offense

Now, this is the second time this week I'm calling out Scoot Henderson, and I genuinely don't want this to become a habit of picking on him.

But we have to talk about what's happening.

In 23 minutes against the Lakers, Scoot finished with two points, two rebounds, and five turnovers. He shot 0-of-2 from the field and struggled to make a meaningful impact offensively.

And I don't want to overreact to one bad performance.

We've already seen Scoot take significant strides in his development. Last season, there were moments when you could genuinely see the player Portland envisioned when it drafted him.

But right now, it feels like he's taken a step backward.

I don't necessarily think he's regressed as a basketball player. I think he's uncomfortable.

More specifically, I think he's uncomfortable with his role and the offensive system he's being asked to operate in.

Portland looks like a considerably different basketball team offensively this season. Under Micah Nori, the Blazers have moved away from some of the midrange-oriented and more free-flowing offense we saw previously.

The emphasis now is much more modern: attack the basket, generate paint touches, and create opportunities from three.

There's considerably less room for the type of improvisational offense that Scoot seemed more comfortable playing in previously.

And I think that's creating a problem.

Scoot plays too damn fast.

He sometimes looks like he's trying to operate in an entirely different offensive environment from the one Portland is actually trying to establish. He wants to push the tempo, make things happen immediately, and attack before the defense has even had a chance to settle.

Think about the Indiana Pacers offense we've seen under Tyrese Haliburton. That team thrives on playing at an incredibly high speed, creating advantages in transition, and forcing defenses to make decisions before they're ready.

Scoot sometimes plays with that same mentality.

But Portland isn't necessarily built to play that way every possession.

With Damian Lillard and Jrue Holiday, two veteran guards who understand the importance of controlling tempo, the Blazers have a different dynamic. They currently rank 12th in offensive pace, and there's a reason for that.

They need to be able to pick their spots.

Scoot has to learn when to push, when to slow down, and when to simply let the offense develop.

Because right now, the physical tools and talent are there. What I'm waiting to see is whether he can find a comfortable role within what Portland is trying to build.

I still believe there's a very good player in there. But the sooner he figures out how to operate within this system, the better.

Portland's Offense Wasn't the Problem. Its Defense Was.

Here's something that makes this loss particularly frustrating if you're a Portland fan.

The Blazers actually played a pretty good offensive game.

They shot 51.4% from the field, knocked down 44.2% of their three-point attempts, and finished with 29 assists.

For comparison, the Lakers shot just 45.2% from the field and 29.7% from three.

Portland also had multiple players contribute offensively.

Deni Avdija finished with 22 points, nine rebounds, and 11 assists. Damian Lillard added 22 points, while Donovan Clingan contributed 17 points and 11 rebounds. Toumani Camara chipped in another 18 points.

That's a pretty productive night from your core rotation.

But here's the problem: the Lakers scored 52 points in the paint compared to Portland's 32.

They also collected 17 offensive rebounds, compared to just eight for Portland.

And then there's the turnover situation.

Portland committed 24 turnovers.

Twenty-four.

The Lakers committed just 11.

That's a 13-turnover difference, and it's almost impossible to overcome that against a team with this much offensive talent.

You can shoot the ball incredibly well, move it around, and generate quality looks, but you're not going to win many games when you're repeatedly giving the opposition additional possessions.

The Lakers also recorded 15 steals, consistently turning Portland's mistakes into opportunities on the other end.

So while I thought Portland did enough offensively to win this basketball game, defensively, it simply wasn't good enough.

And when you're playing against Luka Doncic, defensive mistakes tend to become very expensive.

Damian Lillard Needs to Remember Who He Is

There's another conversation I think Portland needs to have, and it involves Damian Lillard.

I thought Dame had a pretty solid game. He finished with 22 points, shot 5-of-13 from the field, knocked down four threes, and went a perfect 8-of-8 from the free-throw line.

But there's something I've been waiting to see from him that hasn't quite materialized yet.

I haven't seen him take over a fourth quarter the way we're accustomed to seeing him do.

And I'm beginning to wonder whether he's been a little too passive.

I understand the importance of playing within the offense. I understand that Deni Avdija is the future of this franchise, and frankly, he's probably already its best player.

Deni is a tremendous talent. He finished this game just one rebound short of a triple-double, and his ability to create opportunities for everyone around him is becoming increasingly important to Portland.

But there's a difference between acknowledging that Deni is your franchise cornerstone and forgetting what you have in Damian Lillard.

Dame is your closer.

He's the guy who should be comfortable taking responsibility when the game gets tight. He's the guy who should be demanding the basketball and putting pressure on opposing defenses when possessions matter most.

Portland doesn't need him to dominate every possession or completely abandon the team-oriented approach. But it does need him to be aggressive.

Because if the Blazers want to compete at a high level when the postseason arrives, they're going to need the version of Damian Lillard who isn't afraid to take over a game.

And I still believe that player is in there.

The Bigger Picture

I don't think Portland should walk away from this game feeling like it was completely outclassed.

The Blazers scored 118 points against a very good Lakers team, shot the ball exceptionally well, and demonstrated that they have enough offensive firepower to compete.

They even entered the fourth quarter facing a significant deficit and managed to put up 43 points in the final period, making things considerably more interesting than they probably should have been.

But there's a difference between being competitive and being a team capable of consistently winning these games.

Portland has some things to figure out.

Scoot needs to find his footing. Dame needs to embrace his role as the closer. And defensively, this team has to become considerably more disciplined, particularly when it comes to protecting the paint and taking care of the basketball.

As for the Lakers?

Well, they have Luka Doncic.

And right now, that's looking like a pretty good place to start.

Because whatever conversation we're having about the best offensive players in basketball, Luka is making a very serious argument that nobody is particularly close to him right now.

Forty points per game.

Through nine appearances.

And somehow, it still feels like we're getting used to it.

That's the scary part.
